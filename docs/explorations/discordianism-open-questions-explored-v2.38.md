# Discordianism: Open Questions Explored (v2.38): the nineteen families are disjoint, and are nineteen behaviours

*v2.37 proved that each family of 1⇉2⇉3 is rigid. This round proves that the families are pairwise disjoint, over
every nontrivial commutative ring. Together with v2.37, a single Lean theorem says (family, parameter) ↦ census orbit
is injective, so over 𝔽_q at least 19q local-torus orbits are proved.*

*Every separation comes from one invariant: the vertex rows εP of the fold, where ε : kQ → kQ/rad. Their depths along
the radical filtration are invariant under all of Aut(kQ). They show that the 19 families are 19 distinct behaviours.
On 1⇉2⇇3 they answer v2.35's open question about F1–F3: F2 is a different behaviour, and F1 and F3 are mirror images
under the source swap. The audit has 137 results, all on the standard axioms (one on none).*

## 1. Disjointness

The setup: the ideal I_{k,k′} ⊂ ℤ[ga, gb, wa, wb, u, t, t′] is generated by:
- the column-sum relations;
- u·wa·wb·det ga·det gb − 1;
- the 121 entries of h·P^{(k)}_t − P^{(k′)}_t′·h.

Singular gives 1 ∈ I over ℤ for every pair (`analysis/v2_38_disjoint.py`). The self-pairs are the control: there
t − t′ ∈ I, and 1 ∉ I.

The reason is simpler than a Gröbner basis. Each ordered pair has an entry where the two sides are a constant and 0,
whatever h is:

| entries used | count |
|---|---|
| (vertex, vertex) | 250 |
| (vertex, a2b2) | 43 |
| (vertex, a2) | 37 |
| (vertex, b2) | 12 |

Examples:
- families 1 and 4: entry (e₂, b₂) is 1 in h·P¹, and 0 in P⁴·h because row e₂ of P⁴ vanishes;
- families 14 and 17: the vertex blocks differ at (e₁, e₂).

The mechanism: h fixes vertex idempotents and maps arrows to arrows, so ε∘h = ε. Comparing vertex rows of
h·P = P′·h gives εP = εP′∘h, with h block-diagonal on ⟨e⟩, A, B and AB. The vertex block εPι is therefore invariant,
and so is the vanishing of each block of εP. These invariants hold whether or not h is invertible.

So the Lean proof of each of the 342 statements is one `congrFun` and one `simp`
(`lean/gen/gen_disjoint.py`, `Gen/two_pairs_disjoint.lean`). `fam_orbits_injective` does a case split over
Fin 19 × Fin 19: diagonal cases use `fam{k}_rigid_GT` (v2.37), off-diagonal cases use disjointness.

**What is now proved about the census count 659 + 19q:** the lower bound 19q on orbits met by the families, over every
𝔽_q. The upper bound, that the other orbits are 659 at every q, is still computed only at q = 5 and 7.

## 2. τ-strata of the census

τ(P) records:
- the vertex block εPι (symmetric residues);
- for each vertex i and each block X ∈ {A, B, AB}, whether ε_i P|_X ≠ 0 and whether P|_X ι e_i ≠ 0.

It is constant on local-torus orbits, and the script asserts this on every census representative
(`analysis/v2_38_strata.py`).

- **222 strata at both primes.** The first run keyed the vertex block by residues in [0, q), so −1 read as 4 at q = 5
  and 6 at q = 7. That gave 44 spurious strata; the log is kept in `logs/v2_38/`.
- **Families are not τ-constant.** Special members, at t where an entry like t or t + 1 vanishes, fall into other
  strata. The δ_aδ_b families (3, 6, 9, 12) keep all q members in one stratum. The others lose one or two members to
  singleton strata. This is why prediction 4 failed as written: 18 strata contain family points but do not grow.
- **Growth is all family growth, stratum by stratum.** This is a finer form of v2.36's "659 constant": the 659
  non-family orbits are constant in each of their 193 strata. Only 8 strata mix family and non-family orbits.

## 3. An Aut(kQ)-invariant: depths of the vertex rows

Every algebra automorphism φ of kQ preserves rad and its powers, and induces a permutation φ̄ of vertices with
ε∘φ = φ̄∘ε. For φPφ⁻¹:

  ε(φPφ⁻¹) = φ̄·(εP)·φ⁻¹,

so the depth vector d_i(P) = max{n : ε_i P(rad^n) ≠ 0} (−1 if ε_i P = 0) is invariant up to φ̄. Other invariants of
the same kind: rank εP|_{rad^n}, dim(im P ∩ rad^n), dim(ker P ∩ rad^n).

On 1⇉2⇉3, φ̄ = 1. The depth vectors are (`analysis/v2_38_depth.py`, `logs/v2_38/depth.txt`):

| families | depth vectors (d₁, d₂, d₃) |
|---|---|
| 1–9 (k×k, unclassed) | (2,1,−1), (2,2,−1), (1,1,−1), (2,−1,1), (1,−1,2), (1,−1,1), (−1,2,2), (−1,1,2), (−1,1,1) |
| 10–12 (k³, unclassed) | (2,0,1), (1,0,2), (1,0,1) |
| 13 (LRKE) | (0,−1,0) |
| 14–16 (L) | (−1,0,1), (0,1,−1), (0,1,2) |
| 17–19 (R) | (1,0,−1), (−1,1,0), (2,1,0) |

All 19 are distinct. Since each family is a single inner orbit (v2.36), the 19 families are exactly 19 behaviours.

- **Unclassed families.** Their three "triples" are not three behaviour types along three directions, as v2.36 read
  them. They are nine distinct behaviours. Within a triple, the families differ by which vertex rows survive, and how
  deep.
- **Opposite duality.** The depth vectors of v2.36's L ↔ R pairs are reverses of each other: 14 ↔ 17, 15 ↔ 18,
  16 ↔ 19. The same reversal pairs 1 ↔ 8, 2 ↔ 7, 3 ↔ 9, 4 ↔ 5 and 10 ↔ 11, and fixes 6, 12 and 13. This is consistent
  with opposite duality reversing Q and swapping δ_a with δ_b. It is observed in the depth vectors only, not checked on
  the matrices.

**1⇉2⇇3** (`analysis/v2_38_mirror.py`, `logs/v2_38/mirror_pairs_in_7.txt`):
- The v2.36 inner curves match the Lean tables: F1 = rep 63, F2 = rep 129, F3 = rep 242, F4 = rep 338. F1 and F3
  share 5 of 7 orbits with their tables; the other two are presumably the tables' degenerate parameters t ∈ {0, −1}
  (v2.33), which this round did not check. F2 and F4 share 7.
- σ (sources 1 ↔ 3, a_i ↔ b_i) commutes with Δ, maps F1 onto F3, and maps F2 and F4 to themselves.
- Depth vectors: F1 (0, 1, −1), F2 (1, −1, 1), F3 (−1, 1, 0), F4 (0, 1, 0).

This answers v2.35 open question 2:
- modulo Inn ⋊ Sub, the group v2.33 used when it reported Aut(Q) separately, F1, F2 and F3 are three behaviours;
- modulo all of Aut(kQ), F1 = σ·F3, which gives two behaviour types F1 ≅ F3 and F2, plus F4 in class L.

v2.35 noted that the vertex block separates F1–F3 but is not Aut(kQ)-invariant. The depth vector is its invariant
shadow.

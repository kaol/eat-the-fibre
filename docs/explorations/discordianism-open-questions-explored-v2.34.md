## 1. Exact target theorem

**Setting.**
- Q is a finite acyclic quiver, k = 𝔽_q, A = kQ the path algebra, and Δ is arrow deletion, with its ε-rule
  coproduct (v2.1).
- A *fold* is P ∈ End_k(A) with P² = P, im P and ker P subalgebras, and ΔP = (P⊗P)Δ.
- G is the group of arrow substitutions within each parallel class whose matrices have all column sums 1. These are
  exactly the Δ-compatible substitutions.
- The *local torus* at P is the set of class weightings w with P compatible with Δ_w = Σ_c w_c Δ_c (v2.24).
- *Local-torus orbits* are the classes of the equivalence relation on Folds generated by G and local torus moves
  (v2.24, v2.28 §4).
- A *parallel class* is the set of arrows with a given source and target. Its size is its number of arrows.

**Obstruction.**
- Two classes of size ≥ 2 are *consecutive* if they are u ⇉ v and v ⇉ w.
- They are *converging* (or *diverging*) if they are u ⇉ v ⇇ w (or u ⇇ v ⇉ w) with u ≠ w.
- Their *outer ends are joined* if Q has an arrow between u and w.

Q is *obstructed* if it has two consecutive classes of size ≥ 2, or a converging or diverging pair of such classes
with unjoined outer ends.

**Conjecture 3ᶜ (v2.28 §3, the open half).** If Q is not obstructed, the number of local-torus orbits of Folds(𝔽_q Q) is
bounded independently of q.

"Finite" means *bounded in q*. Over a finite field every count is finite, and the census tests constancy of the count
across q.

**The proved half.** If Q is obstructed, the number of local-torus orbits grows without bound in q. This is
Theorems 3 and 4 on every acyclic quiver, with rigidity, **proved (Lean)** (`thm3_general`, `thm4_general`,
`thm3_general_rigid`, `thm4_general_rigid`, v2.30).

## 3. Proof architecture

Write Beh(Q) for the Aut(kQ)-orbits on Folds (v2.33). Write X_P = {φ ∈ Aut(kQ) : φPφ⁻¹ ∈ Folds}.

```
Conjecture 3ᶜ  ⟸  (B) |Beh(Q)| bounded in q                              [behaviour finiteness]
               ∧  (F) for every P, |H \ X_P / Stab(P)| bounded in q     [fibre finiteness; H = census symmetries]
(F) ⟸ (F′) every behaviour fibre is a finite union of H-orbits over k̄   [+ uniformity in q]
(F′) ⟸ every irreducible component of each fibre has an open H-orbit
     ⟸? (second-order, §5) at a generic point of each component, every second-order-unobstructed direction is a
        symmetry direction
```

- **The decomposition is an identity.** The number of census orbits equals Σ over behaviours b of the number of
  orbits in the fibre over b. This follows from transport (§4) and orbit–stabiliser. It is the brief's "decomposition
  principle", now precise: growth in q comes from new behaviours or from wider fibres.
- **The two levels separate.** On every quiver tested, (B) holds whether or not Q is obstructed (v2.33). The
  obstruction acts on (F) alone: Theorems 3 and 4 produce fibres with a one-parameter modulus.
- **(B) and (F) are independent statements.** Neither is proved in general; neither implies the other.

## 4. Strongest new lemma: transport (proved, Lean)

**Lemma (transport).** Let A be a coordinate algebra, and let φ be a multiplicative linear map with two-sided inverse ψ.
φ need not be Δ-compatible. Then

  φ P φ⁻¹ is a fold of A  ⟺  P is a fold of A with Δ replaced by Δ^φ = (ψ ⊗ ψ) ∘ Δ ∘ φ.

**Proof** (`CoordAlg.transport`, `lean/EatTheFibre/Transport.lean`).
- Idempotence, and closure of image and kernel, are transported by any algebra automorphism.
- The Δ condition becomes Δ^φ-compatibility after conjugating by ψ and ψᵀ.
- The twisted algebra has the same structure constants and transported Δ-constants. `twist_delta` gives
  Δ^φ v = ψ · Δ(φv) · ψᵀ.

**Corollaries.**
- Only Δ-compatibility varies along a behaviour.
- The fibre over the behaviour of P is X_P / Stab(P), where X_P = {φ : P is Δ^φ-compatible}.
- Its census orbits are the double cosets H \ X_P / Stab(P).
- **Conjecture 3ᶜ is a statement about the "twist varieties" X_P.** For every P on an unobstructed quiver, only
  boundedly many cosets of coproduct twists Δ^φ are compatible with P.

## 5. Proof or obstruction: the infinitesimal attack, and where it fails

**The strategy.** Linearise everything at each orbit representative P (all exact over 𝔽_p,
`analysis/fibre_rigidity.py`):
- T = T_P Folds, the kernel of the linearised fold equations;
- F = [Der, P] ∩ T, the tangent to the fibre (Der = derivations = Lie Aut(kQ));
- S = [Der_Δ, P] + [E_w, P] with w ∈ W_P, the tangent to the census-symmetry orbit;
- b = dim T − dim F and e = dim F − dim S.

If b = e = 0 at every point, then behaviours and fibres are rigid, and (B), (F′) follow over k̄.

**First order: the strategy fails** (pre-registered predictions 1 and 3 refuted).

| census | representatives | b > 0 | e > 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1⇉2, 1→3 / 𝔽₁₁ (unobstructed) | 137 | 24 | 15 |
| 1⇉2⇇3 + 1→3 / 𝔽₃ (unobstructed) | 407 | 202 | 148 |
| 1⇉2⇇3 / 𝔽₃ (obstructed) | 485 | 224 | 180 |
| 1⇉2⇇3 / 𝔽₅ (obstructed) | 557 | 240 | 220 |
| 1⇉2⇉3 / 𝔽₅ (obstructed) | 1112 | 502 | 342 |

The Zariski tangent spaces of the fold scheme overcount on both sides of the obstruction, so first-order rigidity
cannot be the mechanism. Prediction 2 (obstructed quivers have e > 0 somewhere) holds, but it does not discriminate.

**Second order: a sharp discriminator** (the post hoc refinement, then one out-of-sample test).
- An excess direction [D, P] is kept only if the curve exp(sD + s²D₂ + …)·P stays in Folds to order s² for some
  D₂ ∈ Der. That is a linear condition on D₂ (`second_order`).
- Sanity check: every Δ-compatible symmetry direction passes, at every representative tested.
- The *second-order excess* of a behaviour fibre is the maximum over the fibre of (dim S + extendable excess), minus
  the maximum over the fibre of dim S. A positive value means a direction that survives to second order inside the
  fibre and is not explained by a larger orbit.

| census | behaviour fibres | second-order excess > 0 | which |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1⇉2, 1→3 / 𝔽₁₁ (unobstructed) | 91 | **0** | — |
| 1⇉2⇇3 / 𝔽₃ (obstructed) | 319 | **4** | exactly the four Theorem 3 fibres (width 11) |
| 1⇉2⇇3 / 𝔽₅ (obstructed) | 319 | **4** | the same four (width 21) |
| 1⇉2⇇3 + 1→3 / 𝔽₃ (unobstructed, joined ends; out of sample) | 302 classes → behaviours | **0** | — |

The joined-ends test was run in two stages.
1. On the classes of Inn ⋊ Sub, which omit the substitutions a_i ↦ a_i + λ c b_j, 20 classes showed excess.
2. Their full Aut(kQ)-orbits, computed with those substitutions (`analysis/v2_34/fullorbit.py`), merge them into 18
   behaviours, all with excess 0. The remaining behaviours contain only zero-excess classes, so merging cannot raise
   their excess.

So prediction 4 holds at the behaviour level, and fails at the strictly finer class level.

**Where the argument stops being a proof.**
1. Second-order unobstructedness does not imply integrability. A direction can extend to order 2 and fail at order 3.
2. Excess 0 on the 𝔽_p-points of a fibre is a statement about finitely many points, not about the generic point of
   each component over k̄.
3. Uniformity in q (from finite over k̄ to bounded over 𝔽_q) needs a separate argument.

The missing lemma is local: **(L) on an unobstructed quiver, for every fold P, every Δ-twist direction tangent to X_P
at the identity is, modulo Lie Stab(P), a census-symmetry direction**, i.e. T_e X_P = Lie H + Lie Stab(P). (L) is
linear algebra in the derivations of kQ. Its failure on the Theorem 3 and 4 configurations is exactly the
one-parameter twist found in v2.33 (u = 1 + s·δ_aδ_b, the class scaling, and so on).

## 6. Counterexample report

- **Refuted (first-order rigidity).** Infinitesimal rigidity of behaviours (b = 0) and of fibres (e = 0) fails on
  unobstructed quivers. Minimal example: 1⇉2, 1→3 over 𝔽₁₁, 15 representatives with e = 1. In 5 of them the excess
  direction is obstructed at second order. The other 10 extend, but lead into a larger orbit of the same fibre (the
  fibre-level excess is 0).
- **Refuted (class-level second-order rigidity).** Using classes of Inn ⋊ Sub instead of behaviours gives 20
  apparent excess classes on 1⇉2⇇3 + 1→3. All disappear once the omitted substitutions a_i ↦ a_i + λ c b_j merge
  classes. The lesson: the substitutions along paths parallel to arrows are what the joined-ends arrow contributes.
  That is a concrete foothold for v2.28 §7's question of why the arrow u → w kills Theorem 3's families.
- **No counterexample to Conjecture 3ᶜ** was found.

## 1. The density lemma, equal centres

**Theorem 1 (quadratic case; proved, checked [Z5]).** For any g with deg g = e ≥ 2: k[u²] + k[g] = k[u] **iff** g = αu² + cu + β with c ≠ 0, i.e. a quadratic whose centre differs from 0. After translation, this covers every f of degree 2.

*Proof.* Let σ: u ↦ −u. Then k[u] = k[u²] ⊕ u·k[u²] (even ⊕ odd), so spanning ⟺ the odd parts of the h(g) exhaust u·k[u²].
1. The odd part of h(g) is (h(g) − h(σg))/2 = g_odd · D_h, where D_h = (h(g) − h(σg))/(g − σg). So every odd part is divisible by g_odd.
2. If g_odd = 0, there are no odd parts. If deg g_odd ≥ 3, the odd polynomial u is not reached. So g_odd = cu.
3. Then g = E(u²) + cu and D_h = Σ_k h_k·h_{k−1}(g, σg). Each term has leading term k·E^{k−1}, of degree 2q(k−1) where q = deg_{u²} E. If q ≥ 2 these degrees are strictly increasing, so no cancellation is possible: u² is not in the span, and u³ is not an odd part.
4. Hence q = 1: g is quadratic, and c ≠ 0 means its centre differs from 0.

**Theorem 2 (general equal centres; proved under a congruence condition, checked [Z4]).** Let ψ_f = f^{1/d} and ψ_g = g^{1/e}, both in u + k[[u^{−1}]]. Suppose they first differ at u^{−s}, with coefficient difference δ ≠ 0. (They must differ somewhere, or f and g are powers of a common polynomial and k[f] ∩ k[g] ≠ k.)

Write N = m − 1 at a shared pivot m. Then g^{N/e} − f^{N/d} = ψ_g^N − ψ_f^N = Nδ·u^{N−1−s} + (lower terms). So the eliminated shared constraint is *triangular* in b_{m−1−s}, with coefficient Nδ ≠ 0. That variable can be reserved whenever it is not itself a pivot, i.e. whenever s ≢ −1 (mod d) and s ≢ −1 (mod e). The construction then yields an exact annihilating functional.

Distinct centres is the case s = 0, which recovers v2.4's construction. Checked exactly to order 72 for several pairs, including (u³, u⁴ + u²), (u³ + u, u³ + 2u) and (u³, u⁵ + u³ + u).

**Status of the k[u]-splitting classification (v2.3 Conjecture 4).** Proved, except for the residual class d, e ≥ 3 with equal centres and s ≡ −1 (mod d or e), for example f = u³, g = u⁴ + u (s = 2). For that class there is computational evidence: exact annihilators survive to high order (v2.4 [Y9]).

**Gloss.** *A mirror can share the world only with another mirror. The proof shows this by finding, at every level of detail, a witness that the two orders missed.*

## 2. Edge moduli of the framed fold

**Tangent space (verified [Z1]).** At the framed fold, the infinitesimal deformations are spanned by *single edge coordinates*: the u^{|w|}-coefficient of P(w) for w = a, w = a·uᵐ (left edge) and w = uᵐ·a (right edge), each independent. Nothing else moves: the Hodge (framed words) is fixed. This accounts for v2.4's count of 2c − 1.

**Second order (verified [Z2]).** Write γ for the bare-a coordinate and α_m, β_m for the left and right edge coordinates. At degree 4 there are three obstruction quadrics in the seven edge coordinates (t₀ = γ, then the α's and β's; printed by [Z2]), each of the shape γ·(edge difference) + (edge 2×2 minor), e.g.

  −t₀t₁ + t₀t₂ + t₀t₄ − t₀t₅ + t₁t₅ − t₂t₄

They vanish exactly on the union of three components:

| component | parameters | contains |
|---|---|---|
| **C₀** | constant edges α_m ≡ α, β_m ≡ β; γ free (3-dimensional) | conjugation, γ = α = β |
| **C_L** | any left sequence (α_m); β = γ = 0 | |
| **C_R** | any right sequence (β_m); α = γ = 0 | |

Generic mixtures leave an O(ε²) residual and fail.

**Closed form (verified to degree 6 [Z3]).** On each component the fold is the projection onto the framed Hodge along

  **Podge′ = ⟨ 1, a − γu, uᵐa − β_m u^{m+1}, a·uᵐ − α_m u^{m+1} ⟩**  (subalgebra generated, m ≥ 1)

This subalgebra is complementary to the Hodge in every degree, and it is a coideal. The coideal property is automatic: Δ of each generator lies in 1⊗A + A⊗1, and the Void 1 lies in the Podge. For mixed parameters, the generated subalgebra overflows and meets the Hodge already in degree 3. *That* is the obstruction, made concrete.

**Interpretation.**
- The framed order is held by differences on both sides. Chaos may lean against a word's **left edge with any run-length-dependent weight**, or against its **right edge with any weight**.
- Against **both edges at once**, it may lean only **uniformly**, with an independent push γ on the lone event a.
- The automorphism group G sees only the diagonal α = β = γ.
- The identification fold (v2.4) is rigid because its Podge is an *ideal*, which has no edges to lean on.

**Gloss.** *Eris may push from one side as unevenly as she likes, or from both sides evenly, but never unevenly from both. That is the shape of the freedom an order of framed events allows.*

## 3. The Cayley bijection, and arrow compatibility made combinatorial

**Theorem 3 (proved; verified n ≤ 5 [Z6]).** Splittings of kⁿ into two subalgebras correspond bijectively to forests on the n points in which every tree hangs from one of two roots, **Hodge** or **Podge**. There are 2(n+2)^{n−1} of them (Cayley).

*Construction.* Given a splitting (F₁, F₂), build a bipartite graph G:
- the nodes are the blocks of F₁, the blocks of F₂, and two roots r₁ (no F₁ block) and r₂ (no F₂ block);
- each point v is an edge, joining v's F₁ block (or r₁) to v's F₂ block (or r₂).

The block-indicator matrix is the reduced incidence matrix of G with the roots deleted. Its square case is invertible **iff G is a forest in which every tree contains exactly one root**: this is the multi-rooted matrix-tree theorem, and the bipartite sign change makes the unsigned version equivalent.

Each non-root node then has a unique parent edge. Setting parent(v) = the edge above v's upper node, or the root, gives a forest on the points with roots {r₁, r₂}. The inverse rebuilds nodes as lower endpoints and assigns sides by depth parity.

**Proposition 4 (proved; checked against the numeric criterion for n ≤ 5 [Z7]).** In forest terms, P(e_v) = ±1_{S_v}, where S_v is the union of the Hodge-side blocks in the subtree below edge v.

*Proof.* Solve Mc = e_v up the tree. Coefficients are 0 above v and alternate ±1 below it, and the sides alternate with depth.

So arrow compatibility for i → i+1 is purely combinatorial: **S_i = ∅, or S_{i+1} = ∅, or (S_i, S_{i+1}) = ({k}, {k+1}).** In particular:
- S_v = ∅ iff v hangs from a Podge-side leaf;
- S_v is a singleton iff exactly one Hodge-side leaf sits below v.

Counts for n = 1..6: **2, 6, 23, 100, 563, 3734**. A closed formula is open.

**Gloss.** *Every way of splitting the vertices into order and chaos is a family tree with two ancestors. An arrow survives the split iff, below each of its ends, order is empty or a single leaf in step with the other.*

## 5. New open questions

1. **The last density class.** Equal centres, d, e ≥ 3, s ≡ −1 (mod d or e), e.g. (u³, u⁴ + u). Find the reserved variable one level deeper.
2. **Dihedral rigidity.** Is the dihedral fold rigid, like identification, or edge-soft, like framed? Its Podge is not an ideal, so the edge mechanism may apply. A non-graded deformation theory is needed.

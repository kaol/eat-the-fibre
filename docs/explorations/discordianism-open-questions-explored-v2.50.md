## 1. The theorem

Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic 0. Let L be L_A or L_B, the unique 6-dimensional component of the
rank-drop locus D = {P ∈ V : rank [𝔥′, P] ≤ 5}. Let p be the rational point of L on the v2.44 line (t = 1 for A,
t = −1 for B), and let C = {p + s·v}. Let G = Stab_{Aut(kQ)}(L) be the automorphisms g with g L g⁻¹ = L, and G° its
identity component.

**Theorem (exact one-parameter behaviour moduli on the divisors).**
1. There is a dense open U ⊆ L with U ⊆ G°·C. Every fold in U is conjugate by an automorphism preserving L to a fold on
   the line C.
2. No Aut(kQ)-orbit is dense in L. For all but finitely many c ∈ C, the set Aut(kQ)·c ∩ C is finite.
3. κ is G°-invariant and κ|_C = 1 − s. So distinct points of C, away from finitely many, lie in distinct G°-orbits.

So the behaviours (Aut(kQ)-orbits) on the divisor component form exactly a one-parameter family, for both groups.

**Proof.**

*Step 0, certificates.* L is an affine-linear 6-space defined over ℚ: the Singular prime, and (33a) that L ⊂ V. So L is
smooth and geometrically irreducible. No ℚ-irreducible component of D of dimension 5 can contain a 6-dimensional
geometric component, so L is also the unique 6-dimensional component of D over k. Lean proves that C consists of
folds.

*Step 1, Lie(G) = 𝔤_L = 𝔥′.*
- ⊆: if g ∈ G then g preserves A, since every P ∈ L is a projection onto A. So D ∈ Lie(G) satisfies D(A) ⊆ A, and
  [D, P] ∈ T_P L = L⃗.
- ⊇: for D ∈ 𝔤_L, the vector field P ↦ [D, P] is affine-linear and tangent to the affine subspace L. So its flow
  P ↦ e^{sD} P e^{−sD} preserves L. Hence e^{sD} ∈ G(ℂ) for all s, and D ∈ Lie(G).
- (33b) gives dim 𝔤_L = 6 = dim 𝔥′ with 𝔥′ ⊆ 𝔤_L.

Consequences:
- Ad(G°) preserves 𝔥′, so the rank-drop locus D is G°-stable. This is ChatGPT's "component invariance" qualification,
  which is not needed separately here because G is defined from L.
- [𝔤_L, p] ⊆ [Der, p] ∩ L⃗, and the left side has dimension 5 (33b) while the right side has dimension 5 (33d). So the
  two are equal.

*Step 2, the upper bound (item 1).* Consider Ψ: G° × C → L, (g, c) ↦ g c g⁻¹. Its differential at (1, p) has image
[𝔤_L, p] + k·v. That is 5 + 1 = 6 dimensions, because v ∉ [Der, p] ⊇ [𝔤_L, p] (33e). In characteristic 0 the image of
the irreducible G° × C then has dimension 6 = dim L. So Ψ is dominant, and by Chevalley its image contains a dense
open U.

*Step 3, the lower bound (first half of item 2).* Two standard facts:
- Lie(Aut(kQ)) = Der(kQ).
- In characteristic 0 the orbit map is separable, so T_P(Aut·P) = [Der, P].

Let U₁ = {P ∈ L : rank [Der, P] = 21}. This is open, and it is dense because 21 is the generic rank (33c′). It
contains p.

On U₁, P ↦ [Der, P] is a rank-21 subbundle E of the trivial bundle of matrices, so Z = {P ∈ U₁ : L⃗ ⊆ E_P} is closed in
U₁. Z does not contain p, because dim(L⃗ ∩ [Der, p]) = 5 < 6 (33d). So U₁ \ Z is dense open.

Suppose some orbit O were dense in L. Then O ∩ L, being constructible and dense, contains a dense open W of L. At any
P ∈ W ∩ (U₁ \ Z) we would get L⃗ = T_P W ⊆ T_P O = [Der, P], contradicting P ∉ Z.

This is where prediction 33c mattered. The argument needs p to have the *generic* orbit dimension, not the absolute
maximum 22. The generic rank on L is 21 (33c′), and p attains it.

*Step 4, finiteness on C (second half of item 2).* Let R = {(c, c′) ∈ C × C : c′ ∈ Aut(kQ)·c}; it is constructible.
Suppose the fibre R_c were infinite for infinitely many c. Since C is a curve, some fibre R_c would then contain a dense
open C′ of C. Then Aut(kQ)·c ⊇ G°·C′, which is dense in L, because Ψ(G° × C′) is dense in Ψ(G° × C). This contradicts
Step 3.

*Step 5, the invariant (item 3).* ξ_D κ = 0 for every basis element D of 𝔥′, checked exactly over ℚ(u). So κ is
invariant under G°, which is connected with Lie algebra 𝔥′.

On C (u₀ = −1 + s, the other u = 0): the numerator of κ is (s − 1)·1 and the denominator is −1, so κ = 1 − s. ∎

**What is and is not formal.**
- Formal: Lean proves that C is a family of folds.
- Exact: the varieties, 𝔤_L, the ranks and κ are computed exactly over ℚ (sympy and Singular).
- On paper: the algebraic-group steps (Lie(Stab), dominance, semicontinuity, constructibility).

## 2. The structure behind it

𝔥′ has a 3-dimensional derived algebra. On the [𝔥′, 𝔥′]-invariants, 𝔥′ acts through two characters, coming from the
basis elements 4 and 5.

On V_A the coordinate x = z₄,₄ − z₄,₃ has x − 1 as a semi-invariant with a nonzero character. So its zero set,
L_A = {x = 1}, is 𝔥′-stable. That is the algebraic reason the rank drops exactly on a hyperplane.

On L the semi-invariants u₀(u₃ − u₄ + u₅ + 1) and u₀u₅ ± u₁ − 1 share a character, and their ratio is κ. The same two
factors appear in the generic centraliser vector x(u) of 33h.

## 4. Job 14

The run failed for memory, not for output size. At q = 3 the whole census of 1⇉2⇇3 + 1→3 + 2→4 has 25,622 folds, and
the largest fibre has 1,323. What blows up is the search frontier in the middle columns: the engine's own docstring
records 44 M children (13.4 GiB) at one column over 𝔽₅. Each column's pending generator keeps its parent batch alive,
and there are 18 columns.

A restart as is would fail the same way. Before the next attempt I plan to:
1. Profile in the container: per-column frontier sizes for the heaviest 𝔽₅ fibre, with a time cap, counting only.
2. Based on that, choose between:
   - a column order that prunes earlier;
   - a stronger frontier quotient in the middle columns;
   - a disk-backed stack with within-fibre checkpoints, so that a crash loses less.

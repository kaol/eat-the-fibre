# Discordianism: Open Questions Explored (v2.60): the anatomy of a leaf extension

*Program 3 of ChatGPT's Phase 2 brief asks whether adjoining a single-arrow leaf preserves finiteness, and asks first
for the correct algebraic relation between the folds of Q and of Q⁺. The result is a four-way decomposition of
Folds(Q⁺) relative to the leaf ideal J. Three of the four kinds have a fold of Q attached (the compression), and the
fourth is small.
- **Proved, char-free:** the split kind has an explicit normal form. Its λ = 0 part is exactly the "trivial lifts" of
  Q's folds, so Q's census classes inject into Q⁺'s. Its λ = 1, μ = 0 part exists exactly over the folds that preserve
  the paths into the attaching vertex.
- **Computed** on Q₀ → Q₁ (q = 3, 5, 7) and on eight smaller leaf extensions.
- **Still open:** what is left of a leaf-extension theorem is the fibre finiteness of the λ = 1, μ ≠ 0 lifts and of
  the two one-sided kinds. Those are named precisely here; they are the next step.*

## 1. The setting and the four kinds

Q⁺ = Q + a vertex v and one arrow ℓ between v and a vertex u of Q.
- J is the span of the paths through v. It is a two-sided ideal.
- kQ is a subalgebra.
- kQ⁺ = kQ ⊕ J, and kQ⁺/J ≅ kQ.
- Δ(J) ⊆ J ⊗ kQ⁺ + kQ⁺ ⊗ J, because deleting one arrow from a path through v leaves v in the prefix or the suffix
  (if ℓ is deleted, in the e_v end).
- The leaf arrow is its own parallel class. So the census group of Q⁺ is that of Q, and the weightings are those of Q
  times one 𝔾_m for ℓ. All of these preserve kQ and J.

A fold P of kQ⁺ is of one of four kinds:
- **split:** P(kQ) ⊆ kQ and P(J) ⊆ J;
- **kQ-only:** P(kQ) ⊆ kQ but not P(J) ⊆ J;
- **J-only:** the reverse;
- **neither.**

**Lemma 1 (compression; char-free).** In the first three kinds, the compression P̄ = π P ι is a fold of kQ. Here
π: kQ⁺ → kQ kills J and ι is the inclusion.
- *If P(kQ) ⊆ kQ:* P̄ = P|_{kQ}. It is idempotent, im P̄ = im P ∩ kQ and ker P̄ = ker P ∩ kQ are subalgebras, and
  Δ(kQ) ⊆ kQ ⊗ kQ.
- *If P(J) ⊆ J:* P̄ is the induced map on kQ⁺/J. J is P-stable, so kQ⁺ = im P ⊕ ker P splits along J. Then
  im P̄ = π(im P) and ker P̄ = π(ker P) are images of subalgebras under an algebra map. Δ descends, since Δ(J) is in
  the ideal generated by J in both tensor factors. Applying π ⊗ π to ΔP = (P⊗P)Δ gives Δ P̄ = (P̄⊗P̄)Δ. ∎

The census moves preserve kQ and J, and compatibility with Δ_w descends by the same argument. So compression maps each
census class of these kinds into one census class of Q. The data confirm this: no class of Q⁺ lies over two classes of
Q, in any of the nine extensions below.

**Lemma 2 (trivial lift; char-free).** For every fold P̄ of kQ, P̄ ⊕ 0 (zero on J) is a fold of kQ⁺.
- Its image is im P̄.
- Its kernel is ker P̄ ⊕ J, a subalgebra because J is an ideal.
- For j ∈ J every term of Δj has a tensor factor in J, so (P⊗P)Δj = 0 = Δ(Pj).

So Q's census classes inject into Q⁺'s, as classes of trivial lifts. Compression inverts the map, and moves of Q lift
to moves of Q⁺ with the ℓ-weight 1. ∎

## 2. The split kind (sink leaf ℓ: u → v)

Let B be the span of the paths of Q ending at u, including e_u. Then J = k e_v ⊕ Bℓ.
- Δ(e_v) = 0.
- Δ(pℓ) = (1 ⊗ R_ℓ)Δp + p ⊗ e_v, where R_ℓ is right multiplication by ℓ.
- K ∩ J = k e_v: the e_v-component p ⊗ e_v of Δ(Σ c_p pℓ) vanishes only if all c_p = 0.

**Proposition 3 (normal form; char-free).** Let P = P̄ ⊕ P_J be split. Then:
- P_J(e_v) = λ e_v with λ ∈ {0, 1};
- P_J(pℓ) = φ(p)ℓ + μ(p) e_v, with φ: B → B and μ ∈ B*;
- (*) φ(p) = λ·P̄(p) + Σ_{Δp = Σ pre ⊗ suf} μ(suf)·P̄(pre);
- Δφ(p) = (P̄ ⊗ φ)Δp;
- idempotency: φ² = φ and μ∘φ = (1 − λ)μ.

*Proof.* P e_v lies in ker Δ ∩ J = k e_v, and P is idempotent. Apply Δ to P(pℓ) and expand
(P⊗P)[(1⊗R_ℓ)Δp + p ⊗ e_v]. Comparing the kQ ⊗ e_v components gives (*). Comparing the kQ ⊗ Bℓ components gives the
second identity. Squaring P_J gives the rest. ∎

**Corollary 4 (λ = 0 means the trivial lift).** If λ = 0 then μ∘φ = μ. Induct on the length of p:
- For p = e_u, Δp = 0, so φ(e_u) = 0 and μ(e_u) = μ(φ(e_u)) = 0.
- For longer p, every suffix in Δp is a shorter path ending at u. So μ vanishes on the suffixes, then φ(p) = 0 by (*),
  then μ(p) = μ(φ(p)) = 0.

Hence P_J = 0 and P = P̄ ⊕ 0. ∎

**Proposition 5 (λ = 1, μ = 0: the identity lift).** Then φ = P̄|_B, so P̄(B) ⊆ B is necessary. It is also
sufficient: P̄ ⊕ (e_v ↦ e_v, pℓ ↦ P̄(p)ℓ) is a fold.
- The second identity of Proposition 3 is ΔP̄ = (P̄⊗P̄)Δ restricted to B.
- im P = im P̄ ⊕ k e_v ⊕ (im P̄ ∩ B)ℓ and ker P = ker P̄ ⊕ (ker P̄ ∩ B)ℓ are subalgebras, because kQ·B ⊆ B.

So the identity lifts biject with the folds of Q that preserve B. ∎

**On Q₀ → Q₁** (u = 2, B = ⟨e₂, a, a′, b, b′, cb, cb′⟩, so dim B = 7; `analysis/v2_60_split.py`, `logs/v2_60/split.txt`):

| split part | classes (q = 3, 5, 7) |
|---|---|
| λ = 0 (trivial lifts) | 366 = all of Q₀'s classes |
| λ = 1, μ = 0 (identity lifts) | 228 = exactly the Q₀ classes with P̄(B) ⊆ B (counted independently: 228) |
| λ = 1, μ ≠ 0 | 23 |

The λ = 1, μ ≠ 0 lifts are the only part of the split kind that could carry moduli. Over a fixed P̄, the admissible μ
form a cone in B*. It is cut out by (*), φ(B) ⊆ B, Δφ = (P̄⊗φ)Δ, φ² = φ, μ∘φ = 0 and the closure conditions. The
group acting on it is Stab(P̄) × 𝔾_m(ℓ), and 𝔾_m(ℓ) scales μ. The census sees 23 classes at q = 3, 5 and 7, which
suggests finitely many orbits on each such cone.

**In all nine:**
- the trivial lifts are exactly Q's classes (Lemma 2);
- compression never maps one class of Q⁺ to two classes of Q;
- every class of Q is hit.

**One observation that does not generalise.** On Q₀ → Q₁, P(kQ) ⊆ kQ ⊕ k e_v in every J-only fold, so the mixing
goes through the vertex e₄ alone. This fails when the leaf sits at the source of a parallel class (1⇉2 + 1→3,
1⇛2 + 1→3), and on 1⇉2→3 + 2→4. So it is not a lemma.

The **neither** kind has no compression to Q. On Q₀ → Q₁ it is 16 classes in 6 vertex blocks, all with the leaf
vertex mixed with Q's vertices at the vertex-block level: P(e₄) has components on e₁, e₂ or e₃, and some P(e_i) has
an e₄-component.

## 4. What a leaf-extension theorem now has to prove

Lemmas 1–2 and Propositions 3–5 settle the algebraic relation between the two fold varieties that the brief asked for
first. For finiteness over Q⁺, given finiteness over Q, the remaining obligations are:
1. **Split, λ = 1, μ ≠ 0.** Finitely many orbits of Stab(P̄) × 𝔾_m(ℓ) on the μ-cone of each Q-orbit. This is the
   v2.54 criterion relative to a base orbit; the cone is cut out by explicit equations (§2).
2. **kQ-only and J-only.** The off-diagonal block, Y: J → kQ or X: kQ → J, over a fixed P̄ and a fixed split-type
   diagonal. These need normal forms like Proposition 3.
3. **Neither.** These have no compression. On Q₀ → Q₁ they sit in 6 of the 90 vertex blocks, and that block-level
   handling was already done by v2.57's per-quiver proof. A general argument has to treat them through the vertex
   block, where they mix e_v with Q's vertices.

In every example the fibres are finite on the census side: at most 34 classes of Q⁺ over one class of Q (Q₀ → Q₁).
Item 1 is the natural next computation. Over each of Q₀'s 118 char 0 components, compute the μ-cone exactly and apply
the relative criterion. That gives a char 0 proof of the split part of the extension that is structural, not a rerun of
Q₁'s 90-block pipeline.

**Exact status.**
- *Proved (char-free):* Lemmas 1–2, Proposition 3, Corollary 4, Proposition 5.
- *Computed (census, q = 3, 5, 7 for Q₀ → Q₁; q = 3 and 5 for three of the others, q = 3 for the rest):* the tables.
- *Open:* the fibre finiteness of items 1–3 in general. No counterexample has been seen; any counterexample to the
  leaf lemma between unobstructed quivers would also be a counterexample to Conjecture 3ᶜ.

*v2.35 asked for an invariant separating F1, F2 and F3 on 1⇉2⇇3. v2.38 computed the answer: F2 is its own behaviour,
and F1 and F3 are mirror images under the source swap. This round proves the whole classification in Lean.*

- *Every algebra automorphism of k(1⇉2⇇3) over a domain is filtered, or filtered after the source swap S.*
- *Over a field, for t, t′ ∉ {0, −1}, F_i(t) and F_j(t′) are conjugate by an automorphism iff i = j or
  {i, j} = {F1, F3}.*

## 1. The dichotomy

This is the same argument as v2.40 for 1⇉2⇉3, with one change. 1⇉2⇇3 has two sources, so the vertex block is not
forced to be the identity:
- **Radical preserved:** as before.
- **Sink:** e₁ annihilates rad from the left, so Φ₀₁ = Φ₂₁ = 0.
- **Sources:** e₀ and e₂ annihilate rad from the right, so Φ₁₀ = Φ₁₂ = 0, and Φ₁₁ = 1 from the row sum.
- **The remaining 2×2 block** [[Φ₀₀, Φ₀₂], [Φ₂₀, Φ₂₂]] has idempotent entries, row sums 1, and orthogonal columns
  (from e₀e₂ = 0). No column lies in rad: an idempotent of rad is 0, because rad² = 0.
- **In a domain**, idempotents are 0 or 1, so the block is the identity or the swap.

The pair version (`Mult.cases`) rules out mixed cases. If Φ were `Filt` and S·Ψ were `Filt`, then S = (S·Ψ)·Φ would be
`Filt`, but S's vertex rows are not unit rows.

## 2. The classification

- **Filtered case:** v2.39's depth argument (`thm3_behaviours_distinct`) gives i = j.
- **Swapped case:** Φ·F_i = F_j·Φ becomes (S·Φ)·F_i = (S·F_j·S)·(S·Φ), with S·Φ filtered. The mirror tables have
  depth vectors swapped at the two sources. So F_i must have the depth of F_j's mirror, which allows only
  (F1, F3), (F3, F1), (F2, F2) and (F4, F4). This is 12 cross lemmas, generated by `lean/gen/gen_swap.py`.
- **Existence:**
  - F2 and F4 are single inner orbits (v2.33).
  - F1 uses the v2.33 witness Φ = Inn(1 + x)·T, with T scaling a₂ and b₁ by t/t′ and b₂ by t(t+1)/(t′(t′+1)), and
    x = (t′−t)/t′·a₂ + (t′−t)/(t′(t′+1))·b₂. The order matters: T∘Inn fails, which was checked in sympy before the
    Lean proof.
  - F3 is reached via S.

So, on 1⇉2⇇3 at generic parameters: **F1 ≅ F3, F2 and F4 are the three behaviour types of Theorem 3's moduli.** All
three types carry a one-parameter family of census orbits. The modulus is in the position of the splitting relative to
Δ, not in its algebra (v2.33).

Not covered: t = −1 for F1 and F3 (the witness's pole), and t = 0, where F1 and F3 change depth (v2.39).
